    Visibility is measured pixel by pixel: the fruit is rendered once with the leaves and once without, after the lens distortion. Fully visible means at least half its pixels reach the camera. Labelled uses the training-data rule: at least 30% visible and a box of 8 px or more, drawn around the visible part. Detectable also needs 20 px across and blur under a third of the fruit's width. Updates a moment after you move.

    How the numbers are worked out

    Hikvision mode uses the DS-2DE2A404IWG1-E datasheet: 1/2.8″ sensor, 2560 × 1440, 2.8–12 mm, pan 0–355°, tilt 0–90°. The horizontal angle of view follows the datasheet (99.4° at 2.8 mm, 32.8° at 12 mm) and is interpolated in between. At the long end that angle works out to about 9.5 mm effective on this sensor, and the sim uses the effective value for blur and pixel maths.

    Aperture. The datasheet only gives F1.8 at the wide end. The sim assumes the lens closes to F2.8 at 12 mm and that the camera runs wide open, so in Hikvision mode the aperture follows the zoom.

    Distortion. The wide end has visible barrel distortion, modelled with a one-term division model and fitted so the frame edge matches the datasheet angle. It fades out towards 12 mm. The vertical angle comes out wider than the datasheet's 51.7°, because the datasheet's two angles don't fit one 16:9 lens.

    Pixels per fruit = effective focal length ÷ pixel size × 30 mm ÷ distance, counted on the full 2560 px sensor whatever the render size. 20 px is the rule of thumb for a detector to find a fruit.

    Blur uses the thin-lens circle of confusion plus diffraction at the chosen f-number. "Sharp" means blur under 2 sensor pixels.

    University arches are 40 × 8 m (corrected length) with 14 plots of 4.00 × 3.50 m, 1.00 m walkways between plots as in the email, a 1.00 m central walkway, and 3.00 m free at each end. The line layout inside each plot follows drawings GH-02 and GH-03 (24-10-2026). Fruit plots: line 1 round tomato (8 @ 0.50 m), line 2 cherry tomato (8 @ 0.50 m), line 3 coloured capsicum (10 @ 0.40 m), line 4 cucumber + zucchini (4 + 4 @ 0.50 m), lines 0.875 m apart. Leafy plots: baby kale, rocca and spinach bands 0.60 m wide, then rosemary, thyme and coriander. Plant growth, fruit set, ripening, picking and re-sowing follow the dates in the two production plans. The arch height (2.6 m at the side, 4.2 m at the ridge) is an assumption.

    Dataset generator. The same greenhouse runs in the separate generator file, which renders random camera shots with YOLO labels using exactly the label rule above.

    Straightened view projects every point of the row face (or board) back into the camera image, the same homography used to correct Meera's tilted photos. Far parts of the row stretch over few camera pixels, so they come out soft. That softness is the real cost of a steep angle.
